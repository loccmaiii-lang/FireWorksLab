Intended direction: A quiet, dark "instrument" in the lineage of Unreal, DaVinci and Houdini. The blue-grey chrome is meant to fall back so the only real light on screen is the firework, and mint is meant to signal "live / here / go". The layout has four zones: structure tree and library on the left, the stage in the centre, the inspector on the right, and the timeline under the stage. It uses dense rows of about 28 px, flat near-square controls, single-weight stroke icons, and an accordion inspector whose closed sections show a one-line summary. The aim is calm, engineering-grade density for a technical artist who works in it all day, not a showpiece. In broad strokes it gets there: the screen is restrained and consistent with itself, and only the export button shouts. The problems are in the details and in the lack of an overall system, not in the overall idea.

Contrast with Pentagram / Apple / Teenage Engineering: Pentagram would treat this as an information-design problem. Every column inset, value column and overlay baseline would snap to one modular grid. Type would be limited to two weights and three sizes, with the numbers (time, frame count, exposure) carrying the hierarchy, labels in one quiet tier, and colour used only to show state. Apple, as in Final Cut Pro or Motion, would make the chrome even quieter but easier to read: toolbars split into clear groups with buttons of the same size, segmented controls with an unmistakable selected state, a fixed label column in the inspector, one colour for "selected/now", a timeline that feels like the heavy, hands-on part of the window, and a page switch that obviously looks like tabs. Teenage Engineering would go furthest. The layout would be locked to a grid and lead with numbers, every control would be labelled in the same tiny way, and the single accent would behave like an LED (on means live or current, nothing else). They would also turn one element, the strip of baked frames plus the large time readout, into the product's signature. Against all three, this screen has the right restraint and keeps to its palette, but it lacks their rigour: alignment, type weights and button styles are decided panel by panel, not by one system.

Biggest gaps:
1. The top bar has no hierarchy, and the page switch does not read as navigation. The right side holds seven items (three status dots, 设置, 资源交付 [2], 资源编排, RTX 4070), all the same size, weight and grey, with uneven gaps. 设置 sits about 14 px from 备份 while the other gaps are about 40 px, so it reads as "backup settings". 资源交付 and 资源编排 have no current-page state, no container and no spacing that sets them apart from status text, so you cannot tell which page you are on. The brand line stacks two names in two typefaces (烟花工坊 and FireWorksLab 5.0.3) plus a boxed "2".
2. Mint has too many jobs. The same mint (about #00D29A) is used for the "UE connected" dot, the export-folder dot, the tab underline, the edit outline in the tree, the playhead and the primary export button. When "OK", "current" and "do this" share one colour, the accent stops pointing at anything. The palette can stay exactly as it is; the fix is to give OK states no colour. If mint only ever means "now/current" plus the one primary action, then amber in the header jumps out immediately.
3. Buttons and labels look the same. The text-only buttons (保存, 另存, 还原, 预览设置, 全部折叠, 筛选, 选项, 设置, 复制意见, 快捷键) are typeset exactly like static labels (距离, 已保存 20:31, 序列方式 单次, 1024 px 5 张). On a screen this full of static text, a text-only button needs one consistent sign that it is clickable. The save/export bar alone mixes five kinds of control: icon buttons, a disabled text button, plain text buttons, an amber status readout, and a filled split button.
4. Alignment and spacing are decided case by case:
   - The two overlay groups on the stage sit on different baselines. "PC 30 fps tick 60" is about 11 px lower than "距离 1000 m 预览曝光 1.00 倍", and both sit inside the crop frame, on top of the image.
   - The overlay text is spaced evenly, so label, value and unit do not group. It reads as six equal pieces: 距离 · 1000 · m · 预览曝光 · 1.00 · 倍.
   - In the inspector, the section arrows sit about 5 px from the column line while the titles and breadcrumb start at about 13 px.
   - The summaries of closed sections end at about x=1300, the value-plus-unit column of the open section ends at about x=1313, and the colour-ramp swatch ends at a third edge.
   - Left margins differ in every column: about 15 px in the tree, 12 px in the library and 13 px in the inspector.
5. The type hierarchy is flat and bold is overused. Bold appears on the brand, the tabs, the selected layer, the breadcrumb leaf, every inspector section title and the time readout, so nothing outranks anything else. There are four or five greys only a small step apart. Chinese and Latin text don't match in size or baseline: "Comet 3.0" looks bigger than the Chinese next to it, and the hyphen in "金曜菊-B" floats with loose spacing. The large "2.00 / 5.24 s" readout is right, but "烘焙 50 / 75" next to it drops to label size even though it is the other number that matters in this view.
6. The warning appears in three places in three formats: ⚠ on the tree row, "⚠ 1 层" in the save/export bar, and an amber "未选" in the header. There is no single place that says what is wrong and where to fix it.
7. The library column looks noisy. Every thumbnail is exposed and framed differently: the sparkler is a blown-out blob, the tails are 3 px streaks in a black square, and 金芒菊 fills its frame edge to edge. The status column is grey on grey, and the one status that is the user's own queue (待验收) gets the same white treatment as "新". The last row is cut off mid-character by the status bar, with no fade.
8. The timeline is under-designed for how important it is. The label column stacks "烘焙" over a range readout ("0.35 – 2.85 s [← →] ⟲") squeezed into the ruler row. The "2 s" ruler label is hidden under the playhead flag. Tracks are cut off with "还有 4 行". The strip of baked frames is the best idea on the screen, but it is shown as a thin, low-contrast band.
9. One badge style carries three meanings: a boxed "2" next to the version, a boxed "2" next to 资源交付, and a boxed "?" next to 快捷键.

- A stock stroke-icon set used at its default weight (Lucide-style undo, redo, copy, panel-left, panel-right, maximize, pencil, hash, timer, scan), not matched to the type weight. The "#" and copy icons look heavier than the thin pencil next to them.

- Boxed count badges ("2", "2", "?") added by default rather than by need.
- Search bar + filter dropdown + "collapse all" + accordion with right-aligned grey summaries: standard component-library settings boilerplate. Competent, but anonymous.
- The ⚠ triangle reused as the all-purpose warning mark in three places.
- Spacing set by one gap value everywhere instead of by meaning, which is why the overlay label/value/unit pieces drift apart.

Top 3 to delete / simplify:
1. Simplify the right side of the header into two tiers. Remove the coloured dots from OK states and leave them as dim text; keep colour only for exceptions (备份 未选 in amber). Move 设置 away from the backup status, for example next to the GPU name. Give 资源交付 / 资源编排 a real current-page state and a visible gap or divider from the status items. Keep one badge style for counts, and drop the boxed "?".
2. Delete "⚠ 1 层" from the save/export bar. The tree already flags the layer; if the warning has to block export, put it on the export dropdown or its tooltip. Regroup the bar into three evenly spaced clusters: history (↶ ↷ 还原), file (复制, 保存, 另存) and deliver (导出 ▾). Show 还原 only when there is something to revert, instead of as a permanently disabled grey word.
3. Shrink the type system to two weights and three greys. Use bold only for "current" (selected layer, breadcrumb leaf, current tab) and the time readout. Section titles drop to regular at label level. In the same pass, put the two stage overlay groups on one shared baseline with label, value and unit grouped tightly, and line up the inspector's arrows, titles and right edges to one inset and one value column.

Score: 6.5/10 - The foundation is restrained, keeps to its palette and has some smart touches: corner marks on the stage frame, the strip of baked frames, one-line summaries on closed sections, and a default-position tick on sliders. But the page switch shows no current page, the one accent does six jobs, buttons look like labels, and alignment is off by up to about 11 px across panels, which keeps it below average agency polish.
