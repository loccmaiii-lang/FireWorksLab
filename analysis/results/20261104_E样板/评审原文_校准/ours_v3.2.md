Intended direction: A dark, content-first DCC workstation in the lineage of Unreal, Houdini, DaVinci Resolve and After Effects. The near-black teal-charcoal chrome is meant to recede so the glowing firework becomes the only luminous object on screen. The layout is the classic four-zone production frame: structure and asset library on the left, a hero viewport in the middle, a dense inspector on the right and a dope-sheet timeline along the bottom. One mint-green accent is reserved for commit actions (导出) and the playhead, and amber marks warnings and pending review. The type is restrained, Chinese-first sans at essentially one size, and the controls are utilitarian sliders with right-aligned values and grey units. Several details aim for quiet competence over flash. Collapsed inspector sections carry summary values (第 3 层, 序列时长 7.56 s, 1024 px 5 张), a disabled row explains itself inline (强度为 0 时无效), hidden fields get a "4 项已隐藏 查看" disclosure, and the viewport has cinematic crop marks with burned-in telemetry.

Contrast with Pentagram / Apple / Teenage Engineering: Apple (Final Cut Pro / Motion / Logic) would use one unified toolbar instead of four separate strips. Panes would be separated by tone rather than hairlines, and the screen would have a single selection colour, one SF-Symbols weight throughout, and one activity indicator in place of a row of telemetry. The inspector would have fewer dividers, more generous row rhythm, and fully drawn native toggles and slider knobs. Pentagram would start from a typographic system: one family, three sizes, tabular numerals, and one shared baseline grid locking the layer tree, timeline track labels and inspector labels into common columns. The wordmark and status bar would read as deliberate identity, and arbitrary rules would come out. Teenage Engineering would treat it as an instrument. It would be monochrome with one saturated signal colour that carries a single meaning, use custom-drawn glyphs and monospaced numbers as heroes, and make the transport and timeline the tactile centre of the object. Every control would look pressable and every state legible from across the room. This screen has none of those authored decisions. It is a tidy, consistent in-house tool whose details were assembled rather than designed.

Biggest gaps:
1. The chrome is split into too many horizontal bands. Before the viewport you read a global top bar, a mode, zoom and view-icon strip, and a separate undo, save and export strip. Under the viewport come a transport bar, a filmstrip row and a ruler row, which makes about six strata of chrome around one picture. The top bar also gives live telemetry (UE 已连接, 导出 FWL_Export, ● 备份 未选, RTX 4070) the same weight and colour as navigation destinations (设置, 资源交付, 资源编排), so nothing reads as status versus place.
2. Selection and state are expressed in about five different idioms. The active layer row gets a heavy mint outline box, the library item a flat grey fill, and segmented controls (引擎回放, 适应, 单次) a slate-blue fill. Tabs (效果) use an underline, the export button a solid green fill, and the "on" toggle a whitish knob. There is no single "this is selected" language.
3. The left column carries two jobs. The layer tree and a full effect library are stacked in one 300 px column with a weak divider, so the asset browser (search, category segment, ten thumbnail rows, status words) competes with the structural hierarchy of the effect being edited. The column feels like two apps glued together.
4. Controls are under-crafted. Toggles (水平镜像, 爆后扰动) are tiny, square-knobbed and barely distinguishable on or off. Slider thumbs are hairline ticks that are hard to see or grab. Reset arrows appear sometimes on section headers and sometimes on rows, and an unexplained circled "A" icon floats beside 曝光. The inspector's disclosure chevrons sit jammed against the panel edge with no left inset.
5. Typographic hierarchy is flat. Nearly everything is one size and one weight, with bold as the only lever for section headers. Values and units do not form a clean tabular column. Mid-grey labels and very faint viewport telemetry (PC 30 fps tick 60, 距离 1000 m) are low contrast for a screen people read for eight hours.
6. The timeline lacks presence. The baked-frame filmstrip renders as tiny dots that read like a loading indicator. Track bars and child-track hairlines are too low in contrast to scan. The in/out and loop glyphs are ambiguous, and the lower right of the timeline is empty.

Warning glyphs (⚠) are scattered in three places at once: the tree row, the toolbar's "⚠ 1 层" and the effect's state.
8. Dead space is unmanaged: about 270 px of empty inspector below 输出, and a half-empty timeline track area. Neither is used for summary, preview or breathing room by intent. It is simply left over.
